\section{What is returned after alignment?}
\label{sec:alignment-postprocessing}

BioseqDB assembles the alignment coordinates, strand, score and CIGAR, then
repeats primary and secondary selection
(Section~\ref{sec:chaining-primary-secondary}). The following quantities
answer different questions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Score and edit count.} The score uses the selected aligner's
    match and gap penalties. The edit count is the number of substitutions,
    inserted symbols and deleted symbols on its chosen path. A generic
    CIGAR \texttt{M} operation can include both matches and mismatches, so
    counting edits also requires comparing the corresponding symbols.
  \item \textbf{Divergence.} Matching minimizers give an estimate of the
    fraction of differing bases. This estimate is available before final
    alignment and does not replace the edit count from the CIGAR.
  \item \textbf{Mapping quality (MAPQ).} This estimates confidence in the
    mapping location. Competing chains and repetitive seeds reduce it.
    BioseqDB follows minimap2's estimate and bounds it to 0--60.
    Secondary mappings receive zero. A high alignment score alone does not
    imply high MAPQ when several locations match equally well.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\label{sec:divergence-prefix-counts}
To estimate divergence, BioseqDB forms cumulative counts of query minimizers
by position. Subtracting two counts gives the number in an interval without
sorting the emitted minimizers. This avoids the extra sorting discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:minimizer-divergence-history}.
