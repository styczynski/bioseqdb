\section{Postprocessing}
\label{sec:alignment-postprocessing}

After the alignment is done, BioseqDB computes CIGAR string, extracts all values returned by the final aligner (like exact coordinates, score etc.), and also calculates
some additional values (if they are requested by the user in query):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Divergence} Matching minimizers give an estimate of the fraction of differing bases
  \item \textbf{Mapping quality (MAPQ)} This estimates confidence in the
    mapping locations
\end{itemize}

Minimap defines diverges exactly as mentioned before in Section~\ref{sec:minimizer-divergence-history}:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{n}{m},
  \qquad
  \widehat{d}=1-\exp(-\widehat{\epsilon})
            =1-\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{1/k}.
  \label{eq:postprocessing-divergence}
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the number of minimizers that are part of chains and $m$ is the number of all query minimizers between first match and last match
BioseqDB similarily to what minimap2 does, returns
\(\widehat{d}\), which is approximation of \(\widehat{\epsilon}\) assuming low sequence divergence.
To obtain \(n\) in BioseqDB I uise cumulative counts of minimizers by position, so subtracting two counts gives the number in an interval without
sorting the emitted minimizers (this avoids cirtical problem I found earlier, which made this step thousands of time slower - Section~\ref{sec:minimizer-divergence-history}).


BioseqDB computes MAPQ with the following heuristic, which again, is the same as used in minimap2~\autocite{minimap2_mapq_implementation}.
For a primary region, let \(s,s_0\) be its current and original chaining
scores, \(s_2\) the strongest secondary chaining score, \(c\) its seed count,
and \(a\) the match reward. Let \(d_1,d_2\) be the region's and its strongest
alternative's maximum DP scores (\(d_2=0\) if unavailable), and
\(I=L_{\mathrm{match}}/L_{\mathrm{block}}\) its alignment identity
(zero when \(L_{\mathrm{block}}=0\)). With primary regions \(\mathcal P\),
repetitive query span \(R\), and minimum chaining score \(s_{\min}\), define
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    u&=\frac{\sum_{r\in\mathcal P}s_r}
             {\sum_{r\in\mathcal P}s_r+R},
    &x&=\frac{\max(s_2,s_{\min})}{s_0},\\
    p&=\min\!\left\{u\min\!\left(1,\frac{s}{100}\right),
                       \min\!\left(1,\frac{c}{10}\right)\right\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:mapq-penalties}
\end{equation}
When both DP scores are positive, the score is capped by a second heuristic:
\begin{equation}
  q_{\mathrm{DP}}=\min\!\left\{
    40Ip\left[1-\left(\frac{d_2x}{d_1}\right)^2\right]
      \ln\frac{d_1}{a},\;
    6.02I^2\frac{d_1-d_2}{a}+0.499
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:mapq-dp}
\end{equation}
The available alignment information selects the branch:
\begin{equation}
  q=\begin{cases}
    q_{\mathrm{DP}}, &\text{alignment available and }d_1,d_2>0,\\
    40Ip(1-x)\ln(d_1/a), &\text{alignment available, otherwise},\\
    40p(1-x)\ln s, &\text{no base-level alignment}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:mapq-branches}
\end{equation}
Let \(n_{\mathrm{sub}}\) be the implementation's count of competitive
secondary mappings and \(T(z)\) truncate towards zero. The reported integer is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAPQ}_0=\min\!\left\{60,\max\!\left\{0,
    T(q)-T\!\left(4.343\ln(n_{\mathrm{sub}}+1)+0.499\right)
  \right\}\right\}.
  \label{eq:mapq-final}
\end{equation}
A primary region with a base-level alignment and \(d_1>d_2\) receives
\(\max(1,\mathrm{MAPQ}_0)\); other primary regions receive
\(\mathrm{MAPQ}_0\), and secondary regions receive zero.
