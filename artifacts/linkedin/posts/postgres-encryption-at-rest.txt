“The database is encrypted” leaves quite a few questions unanswered.

What happens to query results, temporary files, WAL and backups? Where are the keys, and can a restored database recover them?

This post follows a specimen record from SQL into storage and describes the protection offered by pg_tde 2.2.2 on Percona Server for PostgreSQL. It keeps the boundaries visible: authorised sessions still read plaintext, WAL needs separate configuration, and a logical dump is another readable copy to protect.

It is a general PostgreSQL example, not a compatibility claim for BioseqDB's table access method.
