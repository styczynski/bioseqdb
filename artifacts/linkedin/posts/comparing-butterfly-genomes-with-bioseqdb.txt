What happens if we put butterfly DNA into PostgreSQL?

I wrote a tutorial comparing two Heliconius species around optix, a gene involved in their red wing patterns. We import a one-million-base region from each species, count A/C/G/T, measure GC content and find matching stretches of DNA.

The sequences come from NCBI through SQL. BioseqDB stores and aligns them inside PostgreSQL; SQLAlchemy sends the queries, and Plotly draws the results. No separate alignment executable is needed.

We start with one match, draw all the retained alignments, then zoom in on optix with gene annotations and local GC measurements. There are a few useful gotchas along the way, from coordinate conventions to reverse-complement matches. Similar GC percentages certainly don't mean the sequences are the same!

The complete Python code, SQL and notebook are available in the examples repository linked from the article.

One setup detail: the current 0.1.0-alpha.1 Docker images and native packages omit the NCBI integration. For this tutorial, build from source with BIOSEQ_BUILD_EXTERNAL_API_CLIENTS=ON; the article explains how.
